Fig. 4 — When it is worth it

You don't teach someone to help you bake when you only need to bake one cake.

Teaching takes longer than doing — once. It pays off on many cakes, or the same cake every week.

Automating a one-off wastes a day. Baking the same cake four hundred times by hand wastes a year — and only looks like work.

Your liaison report is a cake. Same shape every week, same sources, assembled by hand. That is the kind that pays.

Some cakes should never be automated. Your kid's birthday cake — making it is the point.

5.1 — The end product

You must know what "done" looks like.

An agent always makes something.

You can't hand over a standard you never wrote down.

"make it nice" is not an instruction. it's a wish.

Delivered — Rev B

Three tiers, ivory fondant, one piped band on the middle tier. Fresh flowers on top. Sixty even slices, on the table by four.

Exactly as asked

5.2 — The recipe

You must break it into steps.

"Make a cake" is a wish. "Cream butter and sugar, 4 minutes, medium" is an instruction.

Order matters. You can't ice a hot cake.

Checks. "Until golden brown" is a test you can run.

How much detail? — drag it

Too vagueToo detailed

Too vague and it guesses. Too detailed and you're baking by hand. The skill is the middle.

5.3 — The ingredients

You must give it everything it needs.

No ingredients, no cake. And the best baker alive, with no oven, produces batter.

Fig. 7 — The part you cannot hand over

You still have to taste it.

Judgement is the one thing you can't hand over. That is why you still need to know how to bake.

Done properly, this has a name: evals. The same judges, the same score sheet, every batch.

the head chef who hasn't baked anything in five years can no longer tell when it's wrong.

Fig. 8 — Revised org chart

Now everyone is a Manager.

You were judged on your output. Now you are judged on your team's.

Which makes every hard part of management yours: defining done, splitting the work, checking quality, knowing when to do it yourself.

So the three things are not tips. They are a job description.

We are all being promoted to manager — of agents. It is the best promotion most of us will ever get.

You didn't stop baking. You started running the kitchen.

The question isn't whether you can still bake. It's whether you have changed how you work.
